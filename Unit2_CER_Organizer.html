Part II — Build Your Argument (C·E·V·R)

Power & Freedom in a Divided Nation · 1789–1877
Your Question

Did the United States keep its promise of freedom and power to your group between 1789 and 1877?

You have studied how different groups experienced power and freedom during American expansion and the Civil War. Choose one group, study its Source Set, then plan your answer in the four boxes below. The last box turns your thinking into the first sentence of your paragraph.

Step 1 — Choose one group:
White Americans (Set A)African Americans (Set B)Mexican Americans (Set C)Native Americans (Set D)
Step 2 — Plan your argument in the four boxes. You need TWO pieces of evidence, ONE vocabulary term(s), and a paragraph of 5–6 sentences.
1CLAIM
Your answer to the question — did America keep, break, or only partly keep its promise?
Sentence starter: The United States ______ (kept / broke / only partly kept) its promise of freedom and power to ______ …
2EVIDENCE
TWO specific proofs — a law, event, quote, or number from Unit 2 or your Source Set.
Sentence starter: For example, in ______ (year), ______ .
A second example is ______ , when ______ .
Source ___ shows that ______ .
3VOCABULARY
ONE Unit 2 term, used correctly.
Word bank: abolition · secession · Reconstruction · Manifest Destiny · sectionalism · nullification · removal · suffrage · emancipation · compromise
4REASONING → START YOUR THESIS
Put it together in ONE strong sentence: state your answer AND why your evidence proves it. This becomes the first sentence of your paragraph.
Thesis frame: Between 1789 and 1877, ______’s freedom and power ______ (grew / shrank / grew then narrowed), which shows that America ______ its promise, because ______ .
Step 3 — Write your paragraph. Start with your thesis sentence from Box 4, then add your evidence and reasoning.

Part II — Build Your Argument (C·E·V·R)

Power & Freedom in a Divided Nation · 1789–1877
Your Question

Did the United States keep its promise of freedom and power to your group between 1789 and 1877 — and what changed, and what stayed the same?

You have studied how different groups experienced power and freedom during American expansion, the Civil War, and Reconstruction. Choose one group, study its Source Set, then plan your answer in the four boxes below. Your claim must address both change and continuity, and the last box turns your thinking into the first sentence of your argument.

Step 1 — Choose one group:
White Americans (Set A)African Americans (Set B)Mexican Americans (Set C)Native Americans (Set D)
Step 2 — Plan your argument in the four boxes. You need THREE pieces of evidence, TWO vocabulary term(s), and a paragraph of 6–8 sentences.
1CLAIM
An arguable answer that names both what changed AND what stayed the same.
Sentence starter: Although ______ stayed the same, by 1877 the United States had ______ (kept / broken / partly kept) its promise to ______ …
2EVIDENCE
THREE specific proofs — at least ONE from your Source Set, cited by label (e.g., “Source B2”).
Sentence starter: Source ___ (year) shows that ______ .
In ______ (year), ______ ; likewise, ______ .
A third example is ______ .
3VOCABULARY
TWO Unit 2 terms, used correctly and in service of your argument.
Word bank: abolition · secession · Reconstruction · Manifest Destiny · sectionalism · nullification · removal · suffrage · emancipation · compromise
4REASONING → START YOUR THESIS
Combine claim + evidence into ONE precise thesis sentence that names change, continuity, and why the evidence proves it. This opens your paragraph.
Thesis frame: Between 1789 and 1877, ______’s power ______ while ______ stayed the same, proving that America ______ its promise — as seen in ______ and ______ .
Step 3 — Write your paragraph. Start with your thesis sentence from Box 4, then add your evidence and reasoning.
